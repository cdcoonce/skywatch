skywatch

A synthetic sky survey where detectors compete to tell science from glitches.

skywatch is an agent-driven project: an AI agent designs it, builds it and decides how it grows, while Charles Coonce sets the constraints and reviews what ships.

What you are looking at

Each picture is one star on one night. The line is the flux the telescope returned, with instrument-flagged samples drawn as hollow rings. The shaded bands under it are the truth the simulator injected, one colour per kind, which the detectors never see. Below the flux is one lane per detector, showing every span it reported on that star and night, coloured by how the scorer graded it: solid green bounded the event (IoU at least 0.3), hatched touched it, a blue outline corroborates an event the detector had already found, and a red outline matched no truth at all.

What it found

  • Before any detector was wrong, the scorer was: a simple detector looked like it made 312 false alarms, but 281 of them overlapped real events, and once the scoring rules were fixed the same detections gave 34. Build log 0002
  • Before building a search for repeating dips, the project counted what a 3-night survey holds: only 1 of 34 planets in one run and 1 of 25 in the other transited more than once, with a combined signal of just 0.1 and 1.7 against a bar of 7, so it surveyed 20 nights instead. Build log 0007
  • A fix that worked on the sky it was built on did nothing on six skies it had never seen (seeds 21 to 26) and raised false alarms from 6 to 9, so it shipped switched off and unseen "held-out" seeds became a standing rule. Build log 0008
  • A gate that would have removed 28 false claims was shipped switched off, because it would also have lost 18 real transits that no other detector finds. Build log 0010
  • Dividing every star by the field's median cancels sky-wide effects, but when most stars pulsate the median is itself a wobble that gets written into every star, and fixing that cut one run's false planet claims from 14 to 2. Build log 0012
  • A cause written down for a lost planet was invented by a side script that prepared the star differently from the real detector, and on the detector's own path the star's one candidate was dropped by a shape test at 1.57 against a bar of 1.0. Build log 0013
  • A transit search reads a dip at a median 0.65 of its ideal signal, and most of that loss is the honest price of comparing the dip with the rest of its own night; the one loss that looked fixable changed nothing on 34 runs, so that attempt was closed. Build log 0016

How it keeps itself honest

  • The simulator writes the truth when it injects each event, and the detectors never see it; only the scorer does.
  • A change is designed on development seeds and scored once on held-out seeds, only after every threshold is committed, and a bar that fails there is reported instead of re-tuned. The seed ledger
  • Every pull request is reviewed by a second agent that breaks the code one defect at a time and checks the tests go red, and bars are stated in planets, with an allowance of one planet.
  • A failed bar closes the pull request: the change in entry 0016 gained nothing on unseen runs, so it was closed rather than shipped switched off. Build log 0016
